# X-05: 2D radiance cascades over the plan field

**Status:** done as the offline experiment the plan asks for. Branch `gi/x-05`, on `feature/realtime-gi` with
`gi/s2-03` and `gi/s0-02` merged in. Nothing is called from Apex.

**Result in one line:** the cascades carry bounces 2 and later of a room to within the reference's own noise
(mean 1.3/255 on lit texels, against 8.6/255 when those bounces are left out) with 3 levels and no visible leak, but
they do not add detail that a tile layer lacks, and they need the first bounce from somewhere else outdoors.
**Recommended role:** the later bounces of the Direct3D 9 back end (the "feedback" row of design §3.5), fed by that
back end's first gather. Not a near-field layer, and not a replacement for R-02's bounces on the server.

Marks: [V] verified by a harness named in the line, [I] inferred, [O] open.

## The method

**Cascades** (Sannikov, "Radiance Cascades", 2023). Level `i` has probes every `spacing x 2^i` metres, each with
`d0 x 4^i` directions, and covers the distances `t_i .. t_(i+1)` from its probes, `t_i = l0 (4^i - 1) / 3`. A level's
texel is the mean of its 4 child directions; each child is traced over the level's interval and, where the interval
is clear, continued by the next level's texel of the same direction, blended over the 4 next-level probes around.
Every level is one image of the same size; defaults `d0 = 16`, `spacing = 0.25 m`, `l0 = 1 m`, 3 levels (to 21 m).

**The penumbra condition** is what fixes these numbers: a level may be coarse in space because, seen from further
away, light changes slowly from point to point, and must be fine in angle for the same reason reversed. With 2x the
spacing and 4x the directions a level, the arc between two directions at the end of a level's interval stays near
`l0 x 2 pi / (4 d0)`, 0.1 m here: under a probe spacing at every level.

**The bilinear fix.** The plain merge traces a ray from its own probe and hands it to 4 next-level probes, some of
which may stand behind a wall: light leaks. With the fix a ray is traced once to each of the 4 next-level probes,
from this probe's interval start to that probe's interval start, so a ray is only ever continued by a probe it has
reached. It costs 16 traces a texel in place of 4. A gap remains between the end of a ray and the starts of the 4
child directions that continue it, `l0 x pi / (4 d0)` = 4.9 cm at every level; a wall of the field is 2 x 0.75 px =
9.4 cm thick to the trace at 16 px a tile, so the gap cannot straddle it [I, from the two constants; V for the
cases below]. At 32 px a tile the wall is 4.7 cm and `l0` or `d0` must follow.

**The slab: floor and ceiling in flatland.** Flatland has neither, and they carry most of a room's bounce light.
Here a ray of the plan stands for every elevation over its azimuth inside a story 3 m high. For a receiver at height
`h`, the share of its cosine-weighted elevations that are still under a sheet `a` metres above (or below) at plan
distance `t` is `SlabT(a, t) = 2/pi (atan(a / t) + a t / (a^2 + t^2))`. So a sheet with radiosity `B` between `t0`
and `t1` adds `B (SlabT(t0) - SlabT(t1))`, and a wall at `t` adds `B SlabT(t)`. These shares depend only on the
distance from the receiver, and a cascade's interval is a distance from the receiver, so they are applied at the
nominal distance and nothing has to be carried from level to level. One ray serves three receivers (the texel's
three channels): a vertical face 1 m over the floor (the directional layers of design §3.3), the floor, and the
ceiling. The result is exact for a prism: full-height walls with radiosity uniform over their height [V: a closed
room of unit radiosity returns 1.0000 on all six outputs with whole rays, 0.983 to 1.000 with 3 levels].

**Inputs.** The plan field of S0-06 and S2-03, one band (band 1, "window height": doors and windows open, half walls
gone), and an emitter image the size of the field: per pixel the radiosity of the nearest wall on this pixel's side,
of the floor and of the ceiling. A hit reads the wall 1.5 px back along the ray, so a thin wall keeps two sides.
**Output** (`Gather`): per level-0 probe the irradiance of faces +x, -x, +z, -z at 1 m, of the floor and of the
ceiling. That is the layout of the indirect layer plus the directional layers.

**Bounces** are feedback: the output, times the reflectance, is the next emitter image.

## Results

### The kernel against whole rays (`run_plan_cascades.ps1`) [V]

| Doorway: light behind it, sum / whole rays | 0.998, mean error 0.12 % of the peak | 1.132, 0.41 % | 1.034, 0.78 % |

Ringing, a bright floor and ceiling patch in the open, the cascades over whole rays on rings around it
(mean, lowest .. highest probe):

| Patch | 1.5 m | 3 m | 6 m | 10 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 m (a lamp's pool of light) | 1.01 (0.99 .. 1.04) | 0.96 (0.92 .. 0.99) | 0.98 (0.96 .. 1.00) | 0.99 (0.94 .. 1.07) |

- [V] Most of the ringing first seen was not the cascades but the trace: the sheets are read once a step, and a
  step longer than the source meets it on some rays and misses it on others. Steps are therefore capped at 0.1 of
  the distance from the probe (8 px near it); with 0.05 the 0.5 m patch stays within 0.81 .. 1.18 at 10 m.
- [V] What is left is the cascades' own: a source much smaller than a level's probe spacing rings beyond that
  level's start. Emitters here are lit walls and floors, metres wide; the lamp itself is not an emitter of the
  cascades. A window or a screen as a small bright emitter would ring.

### Leak (sealed rooms, values outside the lamp's own room) [V]

| Arm | Lit | Largest |
|---|---|---|
| Reference | 0 of 12 120 | 0 |
| Fix, bounces 2+ | 6 029 | 0.003/255 |
| Fix, all bounces | 7 367 | 0.007/255 |
| No fix, bounces 2+ | 8 040 | 0.67/255 |
| No fix, all bounces | 8 040 | 2.17/255 |

- [V] Without the fix a closed room next door receives up to 2/255: visible in a dark room. With the fix the leak is
  300 times smaller and under any display step, but it is **not the exact 0** design §3.5 asks for.
- [V] The cause is in the plan field, not in the cascades (traced step by step on the doorway case, outside corner
  (1, 1)): `SeedBand` keeps one seed a pixel, the one nearest the pixel's centre. In the pixel where two walls meet,
  one wall's seed is dropped, and the pixels beside the corner read too far: 0.79 px at a point 0.03 px
  behind the wall line, where a straight wall reads 0.5. A ray entering within about 0.3 px of the corner passes
  the wall line and is stopped a quarter pixel later; `PlanTrace` still blocks it, but the cascades have read the floor inside by then. Along a straight wall and
  at a T junction the count is exactly 0 (the closed wall case).
- [I] The sealed scene's leak is the same effect at its outer corners; most of its lit values are outdoor texels.

### Detail: does it add what a tile layer lacks? [V]

The floor of the two rooms at 4 points a metre, against the same path tracer at those points (512 samples):

| Source | Mean error, lit | Within 0.5 m of a wall |
|---|---|---|
| The reference's tile texels, bilinear inside the room | 1.54/255 | 1.81 |
| A first bounce at 4 a metre + cascades for bounces 2+ | 1.43 | 1.59 |
| Cascades for all bounces | 2.41 | - |

The gain over a bilinear read of one texel a tile is 0.1/255 on the mean and 0.2/255 beside walls, inside the
reference's noise (about 1.2/255). Bounce light at floor level is smooth at the scale of a tile, and what is not
smooth (contact darkening under furniture and in corners) is not in the plan at all. **A cascade detail layer next
to the per-lamp texel layers is not worth building.** Contact detail stays with the AO slices (S5-03).

### Cost by level count

A 64 x 64 tile story of 8 x 8 tile rooms with doors, field 1028 x 1028, an RTX 30 laptop GPU. [O] The machine was
running other GPU and CPU work and the numbers moved by up to 10 times between runs; take the order of magnitude.

| Setting | Image a level | Traces a level | 1 level | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 m probes, fix, ps_3_0 | 1024 x 1024 | 16.8 M | 7 - 11 ms | 12 - 94 | 26 - 84 | 68 - 133 |
| 0.25 m probes, plain, ps_3_0 | 1024 x 1024 | 4.2 M | 1 | 4 | 8 - 9 | 12 - 15 |
| **0.5 m probes, fix, ps_3_0** | 512 x 512 | 4.2 M | 0.6 - 0.8 | **4** | 12 - 52 | 18 - 59 |
| 0.5 m probes, plain, ps_3_0 | 512 x 512 | 1.0 M | 0.6 - 0.7 | 2 - 3 | 4 - 5 | 7 - 8 |
| 0.25 m probes, fix, compute | 1024 x 1024 | 16.8 M | 10 | 50 | 25 | 77 |
| 0.5 m probes, fix, compute | 512 x 512 | 4.2 M | 3 | 68 | 56 | 22 |

- [V] Every level has the same number of texels and traces; a further level costs about the same again, and its
  traces are longer.
- [I] One solve of a large story at 0.5 m probes and 2 levels (10 m, enough for a room) is about 4 ms; 3 bounces
  are 3 solves. The ps_3_0 form is already 16 passes a level, each under 1 ms, so it slices into the 2 ms a frame of
  design §7 as it is. The cost does not depend on the number of lamps only if all lamps share one coloured solve;
  with one layer a lamp it is a solve a lamp, on change of that lamp only.
- Memory: one RGBA16F image a level, 2 MB at 512 x 512, plus the emitter image at field size (8.5 MB for the
  1028 x 1028 field), all on the server under the bridge.

## Recommendation

| Role | Verdict | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Later bounces of the **Direct3D 9 back end** (design §3.5, "Feedback, 2-3 passes"), after its first gather | **Adopt as the candidate**, 0.5 m probes, `l0` 2 m, 2 levels, fix | 8.6 -> 1.3/255 indoors, 2.0 -> 0.9 on the house; about 4 ms a solve |
| Replace bounces 2+ of R-02 on the server | **No** | R-02's bounces are gathers over stored links with no ray, see furniture and all stories, and settle in 13 - 17 passes; the cascades would be an approximation of something already cheap and exact |
| The whole indirect solve of the Direct3D 9 back end, first bounce included | Indoors only, and only with a direct-light pass a field pixel that includes ceilings | 1.9/255 indoors; 18 % of the light missing outdoors |
| Near-field or detail layer beside the texel layers | **No** | 1 m of reach recovers a fifth; detail gain 0.1 - 0.2/255, inside noise |

What the first row replaces is the repeat of the clustered virtual-light gather for bounce 2 and 3, whose cost grows
with lamps times virtual lights; the cascades' cost is fixed a story. What it needs from S2-02 is the first-bounce
layer in the five-face layout it already produces, plus a ceiling value (the floor's is a usable stand-in for bounce
2 and later: that is what the "bounces 2+" rows above were run with).

## Open issues

- **Plan field corner seeds** (S0-06, S2-03): keep both walls' seeds where two walls share a pixel, or the
  diagonal pixel reads too far. Until then "exactly 0 through a closed wall" holds along walls and not within a
  third of a pixel of an outside corner. In the cascades the leak can also be removed locally by adding a step's
  sheet light only once the next step has not hit. Not done.
- The emitter image is built on the CPU in the harness. In Apex it is one pass over the field: nearest tile's
  layer value times reflectance, the wall's side from the field's gradient (`CascadesRef::EmitFaces` is that pass on
  the CPU).
- One band: a door's head and a window's sill are open over the whole height, half walls are gone. Blending the
  bands by the share of the story's height each covers was not tried.
- One story: no light between stories, none from the ground to an upper story's outdoor faces.
- One reflectance (0.5), white light. A coloured solve is the same passes on three images, or one image a channel.
- Timings need a quiet machine (with R-G1). The DXVK run of the ps_3_0 form is missing.
- `tools/rt_lamps_test/check.cpp`'s default (device) mode was not run on this branch; `--pathref` and `--cascades`
  were.
- Reference: S0-02's scenes only; no captured lot.
